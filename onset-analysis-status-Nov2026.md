Proposal for India

  - Use smoothed deviance explained statistic (in conjunction with agricultural and logistical considerations) 
  to expand region for monsoon onset prediction
  - Also consider predicting monsoon cessation (in regions determined in similar fashion)
  - Winter (fall) monsoon onset in southeastern India not a good target for prediction:
    - Predictability based on deviance explained appears to be quite low
    - Areas where there is some degree of predictability are very limited in spatial extent (just a small area of Tamil Nadu opposite Sri Lanka)

Discussion points for Ethiopia

  - Would a similar strategy for Kiremt (JJAS) onset (and possibly cessation) be useful?
    - Would expanding prediction region further to west/northwest be useful
    - Southern woredas don't show much predictability according to statistical approach - how well does current forecasting approach perform there?
  - Discuss empirical findings:
    - Gap between Belg and Kiremt rains often seems minimal/non-existent
    - Clear onset often occurring very early in the year, with changepoint analysis often finding start of Belg (see plot below)
    - Statistical approach is therefore primarily assessing whether there is clear onset without distinguishing Belg and Kiremt
    - With current forecasting approach, the results suggest that forecasting window may be "artificially" starting after at least moderate rainfall has started (see plot below)? Should window expand to include May (and perhaps into April)?
